Home Sale Prices by Municipality

What is this dashboard?

It shows the typical price paid for a property in each of Allegheny County's municipalities (and each ward of Pittsburgh, McKeesport, Clairton and Duquesne), every year since 2012. It counts only sales the County codes as valid, of a single parcel. "Typical" is the median: half the sales were for less, half for more.

What the data shows

    How to use it

    • Municipality: find your municipality or ward on the map, see its price every year, and compare it with up to five others.
    • County Summary: how prices moved across the County, and whether cheap and expensive places moved alike.
    • Data and Definitions: what counts as a sale, what the numbers mean, and how far each year can be trusted.

    What data is available?

    Definitions

    Typical price
    The median price of a place's valid sales in a year: half sold for less, half for more.
    Middle half
    The range from the 25th to the 75th percentile price: the middle 50% of sales.
    Valid sale
    A sale the County codes as valid (codes 0, U, UR), with a price, whose deed covers one parcel on one sale date. Sales between relatives, sheriff sales and other non-market transfers are coded otherwise and not counted.
    Municipality
    The County's municipality code. It splits Pittsburgh, McKeesport, Clairton and Duquesne into wards, so a ward counts as one place.
    Typical municipality
    The median of the medians of all places with enough sales that year: a middle-of-the-road place, not a countywide average.
    Typical yearly change
    Measured inside each place (this year's median ÷ last year's) for places with enough sales in both years, then the median of those changes. A change in which places have enough sales can't create a change on its own.
    Few sales
    Fewer than 10 valid sales. No median is shown with fewer than 5.
    Provisional
    The current year: it isn't finished, and the County is still reviewing its sale codes. Its numbers will change.
